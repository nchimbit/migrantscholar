**What displaced learners need to know to access Türkiye Scholarships**

If you’re a migrant, refugee, asylum seeker, or international student looking to study in Turkey, the Türkiye Scholarships programme is one of the most comprehensive options available. This guide explains the basics of the scholarship, who can apply, what support is offered, and how to prepare a strong application. It is written for displaced learners who want a clear, accurate overview without any unverified claims.

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

### General Eligibility

1. **Nationality** – The scholarship is open to students from any country, including those who have been granted refugee status or are in a displacement situation.
2. **Academic record** – Applicants must have a strong academic background. For undergraduate programmes, a high school diploma or equivalent is required; for graduate programmes, a bachelor’s degree (or equivalent) is necessary.
3. **Language proficiency** – Most programmes are taught in Turkish or English. Applicants must provide proof of proficiency (e.g., TOEFL, IELTS, or Turkish language test) unless the programme is offered in the applicant’s native language.
4. **Age limits** – Some programmes have upper age limits, especially for undergraduate studies. Check the specific programme page for details.

### Additional Requirements for Displaced Learners

- **Proof of displacement** – Refugee status, asylum application, or a document confirming displacement may be required. The scholarship office can provide guidance on acceptable documents.
- **Health insurance** – All applicants must have valid health insurance covering the duration of their studies. The scholarship usually includes a health insurance component, but proof of coverage may still be needed.
- **Financial self‑sufficiency** – While the scholarship covers tuition and living costs, applicants may need to demonstrate that they have no outstanding debts or obligations that could interfere with their studies.

> **Tip:** Keep copies of all documents in both digital and hard‑copy form. The scholarship office may request originals or notarised copies.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

The Türkiye Scholarships programme is designed to be fully funded, but the exact components can differ by study level and field of study:

- **Tuition fees** – Covered for the entire duration of the programme.
- **Living stipend** – Monthly allowance to cover accommodation, food, transport, and other living expenses.
- **Health insurance** – Comprehensive coverage for the duration of the scholarship.
- **Travel allowance** – One‑time reimbursement for travel to and from Turkey.
- **Language training** – Some programmes include Turkish language courses for international students.
- **Academic support** – Access to university libraries, research facilities, and academic advising.

> **Note:** The specific amounts and coverage details are updated annually. Check the official website for the most recent figures.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Identify the Right Programme

- Browse the scholarship portal and filter by study level, field of study, and language of instruction.
- Read the programme description carefully to confirm that it aligns with your academic goals and displacement status.

### 2. Gather Academic Credentials

- Transcripts, diplomas, and certificates from previous schools or universities.
- Official translations if documents are not in Turkish or English.
- A statement of purpose or research proposal (for graduate programmes).

### 3. Prepare Language Proficiency Proof

- Take a recognised language test (TOEFL, IELTS, or Turkish language test) if required.
- Keep the official score report and any additional language certificates.

### 4. Compile Personal Documents

- Passport copy (valid for at least six months beyond the intended study period).
- Refugee or asylum status documents (if applicable).
- Health insurance proof.
- Any additional documents requested by the specific programme.

### 5. Draft a Strong Personal Statement

- Highlight your academic achievements, research interests, and how the scholarship will help you achieve your goals.
- For displaced learners, explain how your displacement has shaped your aspirations and how studying in Turkey will contribute to your community or personal development.

### 6. Secure Letters of Recommendation

- Obtain at least two letters from teachers, professors, or employers who can attest to your academic abilities and character.
- Provide the recommenders with a brief overview of the scholarship and the programme to help them tailor their letters.

### 7. Review the Application Checklist

- Double‑check that all required documents are included.
- Ensure that all forms are filled out correctly and signed where necessary.
- Verify that the application fee (if any) has been paid.

### 8. Submit the Application

- Most applications are submitted online through the scholarship portal.
- Keep a confirmation email or screenshot of the submission as proof.

### 9. Follow Up

- After submission, you may receive a confirmation of receipt.
- If additional documents are requested, submit them promptly.
- Keep track of any interview or assessment dates.

> **Caution:** The scholarship office may contact you for additional information. Respond in a timely and professional manner.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Submitting incomplete applications** – Missing documents or incomplete forms can lead to automatic rejection. Double‑check the checklist before submitting.
2. **Using informal language in the personal statement** – Maintain a professional tone and avoid slang or overly casual phrasing.
3. **Failing to proofread** – Spelling or grammatical errors can undermine the perceived quality of your application. Have someone else review it.
4. **Ignoring language requirements** – Even if you are comfortable in Turkish, some programmes require proof of English proficiency (or vice versa). Verify the language criteria.
5. **Not following the application deadline** – Late submissions are usually not accepted. Set reminders for key dates.
6. **Assuming the scholarship covers all expenses** – While the scholarship is fully funded, some incidental costs (e.g., travel to Turkey, certain course materials) may not be covered. Plan accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. **Can refugees apply for Türkiye Scholarships?**

Yes, refugees and displaced learners are eligible to apply, provided they meet the academic and language requirements. You may need to provide documentation confirming your refugee status.

### 2. **Do I need a Turkish visa to study in Turkey?**

All scholarship recipients must obtain a student visa. The scholarship office can guide you through the visa application process, but you should also consult the Turkish embassy or consulate in your country for specific visa requirements.

### 3. **What if I do not speak Turkish?**

Many programmes are offered in English. If you choose a Turkish‑taught programme, you may need to complete a Turkish language course before or during your studies. Check the programme page for language prerequisites.

### 4. **Is the scholarship renewable if I need to extend my studies?**

Renewal policies vary by programme and level. Some scholarships allow extensions for additional semesters or years, while others are fixed. Discuss renewal options with the scholarship office during your application.

### 5. **Can I work part‑time while studying with a Türkiye Scholarship?**

Scholarship recipients are generally allowed to work part‑time, but the amount of work hours may be limited. Verify the work‑permit regulations with the scholarship office and local authorities.

### 6. **What happens if I fail a semester?**

Academic performance is monitored. If you fail a semester, you may need to retake the course or meet with an academic advisor. The scholarship office can advise on the specific consequences for your programme.
