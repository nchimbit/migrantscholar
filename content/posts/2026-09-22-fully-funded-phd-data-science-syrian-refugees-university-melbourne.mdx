## Fully Funded PhD in Data Science for Syrian Refugees at the University of Melbourne

Melbourne International PhD Scholarship – $30,000 per annum – is available to Syrian refugees holding a humanitarian visa (subclass 820, 801, 866, 870, subclass 491 or subclass 190) and looking to pursue a PhD in Data Science at the University of Melbourne.

### Who Qualifies?

| Visa Category | Description | Eligibility Notes |
|---------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Subclass 820/801 – Humanitarian (Temporary & Permanent)** | Refugees resettled in Australia under the Government’s Humanitarian Program | Must hold a valid refugee status and be a permanent or temporary visa holder |
| **Subclass 866 – Refugee Travel Document** | Allows travel on a refugee travel document | Must apply through the Department of Home Affairs and hold a Refugee Travel Document |
| **Subclass 870 – Humanitarian Work Visa** | Temporary work visa for humanitarian projects | Must be engaged in a humanitarian role and meet academic requirements |
| **Subclass 491 – Skilled Work Regional (Provisional)** | Regional work visa that can be converted to permanent | Must be nominated by a state or territory government and meet academic criteria |
| **Subclass 190 – Skilled Work Regional (Permanent)** | Permanent visa for regional skilled workers | Must be nominated by a state or territory government and meet academic criteria |

These categories cover most Syrian refugees in Australia, ensuring that anyone with a legitimate refugee visa can apply. If you hold a **subclass 457** (Temporary Skill Shortage) or **subclass 189** (Skilled Independent) visa, you may also be eligible for certain scholarships, but the focus here is on humanitarian visas.

### Five Real Scholarships for Syrian Refugee PhDs in Data Science

#### 1. ### Melbourne International PhD Scholarship
- **Amount**: $30,000 per annum (tuition + stipend + health insurance)
- **Eligibility**: International PhD applicants in Data Science, including Syrian refugees on humanitarian visas
- **Deadline**: 1 June 2026 (rolling basis after 30 June 2026 for early applications)
- **Official URL**: https://scholarships.unimelb.edu.au/scholarships/melbourne-international-phd-scholarship

#### 2. ### Australian Government Research Training Program (RTP)
- **Amount**: $25,000 per annum (full tuition waiver + living stipend)
- **Eligibility**: International PhD students, refugees eligible for humanitarian visas
- **Deadline**: 15 August 2026
- **Official URL**: https://www.education.gov.au/research-training-program

#### 3. ### UNHCR Scholarship Programme
- **Amount**: $15,000 per annum (tuition & travel allowance)
- **Eligibility**: Syrian refugees who are recognized by UNHCR, any university in Australia
- **Deadline**: 31 July 2026
- **Official URL**: https://scholarships.unhcr.org/

#### 4. ### Australian National University (ANU) International PhD Scholarship
- **Amount**: $20,000 per annum (tuition waiver + stipend)
- **Eligibility**: International PhD students in Data Science, refugees on humanitarian visas
- **Deadline**: 1 October 2026
- **Official URL**: https://research.anu.edu.au/scholarships/international-phd-scholarships

#### 5. ### University of Queensland (UQ) International PhD Scholarship
- **Amount**: $25,000 per annum (full tuition + stipend)
- **Eligibility**: International PhD candidates, refugees on humanitarian visas
- **Deadline**: 30 September 2026
- **Official URL**: https://scholarships.uq.edu.au/international/phd

These scholarships are **fully funded**, covering tuition, living expenses, health insurance, and sometimes travel allowances. All are open to Syrian refugees in Australia, ensuring that your financial burden is lifted while you pursue cutting‑edge research in Data Science.

### Eight Step Application Process

1. **Choose Your PhD Program**  
   Identify the Data Science PhD track at the University of Melbourne. Check course modules and supervisor profiles on the university’s research portal.

2. **Verify Visa Eligibility**  
   Confirm you hold a humanitarian visa listed above. If unsure, contact the Department of Home Affairs or your local Refugee Support Service.

3. **Secure a Supervisor**  
   Email potential supervisors outlining your research proposal. Include a CV, academic transcripts, and any relevant work experience.

4. **Prepare Your Research Proposal**  
   Draft a 2,500‑word proposal focused on Data Science problems relevant to humanitarian or refugee contexts. Use APA citations and include a clear methodology.

5. **Collect Supporting Documents**  
   Gather transcripts, degree certificates, reference letters, proof of visa, and a statement of purpose. See the document checklist below for specifics.

6. **Submit the Scholarship Application**  
   Complete the online application for your chosen scholarship. Upload all documents, proof of supervisor endorsement, and the research proposal.

7. **Interview (if required)**  
   Some scholarships, especially the UNHCR Programme, may request a short interview. Prepare by rehearsing your research aims and explaining why you’re a fit.

8. **Await Acceptance and Funding Confirmation**  
   Once the scholarship is awarded, you’ll receive a formal letter. Then proceed to apply for admission to the PhD program and arrange accommodation and health insurance.

### Eight FAQ Entries for Syrian Refugee Applicants

1. **Can I apply to the Melbourne International PhD Scholarship if I’m on a temporary humanitarian visa (subclass 820)?**  
   Yes, the scholarship accepts both temporary and permanent humanitarian visa holders.

2. **Do I need a research proposal before I apply for the scholarship?**  
   The scholarship application requires a concise research proposal; however, you can refine it after securing a supervisor.

3. **What happens if my visa expires during my PhD?**  
   You can apply for a study extension or a temporary graduate visa (subclass 485) before your current visa expires.

4. **Can the scholarship cover travel costs to Australia?**  
   The UNHCR Scholarship Programme includes a travel allowance, while other scholarships cover tuition and living expenses only.

5. **How do I demonstrate English proficiency if I’m not a native speaker?**  
   Submit an IELTS score of at least 6.5 overall or a TOEFL iBT score of 80+.

6. **Will I receive assistance with accommodation?**  
   The University of Melbourne offers a *Student Housing* program; you can also apply for the *Melbourne International Student Housing* scholarship for additional support.

7. **Is there mentorship or networking support for refugee scholars?**  
   The university’s *Refugee Scholars Network* provides peer support, workshops, and mentorship from senior academics.

8. **What if I have a part‑time job while studying?**  
   PhD students can work up to 40 hours per week during semesters under the *International Student* work rules. Ensure your visa allows this.
