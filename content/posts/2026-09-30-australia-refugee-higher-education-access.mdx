This guide is written for refugees, asylum seekers, and humanitarian entrants who are considering higher education in Australia. It is also useful for family members, community workers, and educators who support refugees in navigating the Australian education system.

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

### 1. Visa Pathways

| Visa type | Purpose | Key Points for Refugees |
|-----------|---------|------------------------|
| **Refugee visa (subclass 200)** | Permanent protection | Refugees granted this visa may apply for a student visa (subclass 500) after acceptance to a course. |
| **Humanitarian visa (subclass 200/201)** | Permanent protection | Similar pathway to the refugee visa. |
| **Student visa (subclass 500)** | Study in Australia | Required for all international students, including refugees. Must hold an Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from an Australian education provider. |

**Important requirements for the student visa**  
- **English proficiency**: IELTS 5.5 overall (or equivalent) is a common requirement.  
- **Health insurance**: Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory.  
- **Financial capacity**: Proof of sufficient funds to cover tuition, living expenses, and return travel.  
- **Character**: Police clearance may be required.  

### 2. Academic Eligibility

- **Previous study**: Most universities require completion of secondary education or an equivalent qualification.  
- **English language**: Many courses require a minimum English test score.  
- **Course prerequisites**: Some programs have specific subject requirements (e.g., mathematics for engineering).  

### 3. Scholarship Eligibility

- **Australia Awards Scholarship**: Open to refugees and humanitarian entrants from eligible countries.  
- **University‑specific scholarships**: Many Australian universities offer scholarships that explicitly welcome refugees and humanitarian entrants.  
- **General criteria**: Academic merit, leadership potential, community involvement, and financial need are common factors.

## How to Prepare an Application

1. **Research courses and universities**  
   - Use the Australian Government’s *Study in Australia* portal to compare courses.  
   - Check each university’s website for specific entry requirements.  

2. **Check visa eligibility**  
   - Confirm you hold a valid refugee or humanitarian visa.  
   - Review the *Student visa* requirements on the Department of Home Affairs website.  

3. **Gather academic records**  
   - Obtain certified copies of secondary school transcripts.  
   - Translate documents into English if necessary.  

4. **Prepare English proficiency evidence**  
   - Take an IELTS, TOEFL, or equivalent test.  
   - Some universities accept alternative proof such as a completed English language course.  

5. **Apply for scholarships**  
   - Identify scholarships that match your profile.  
   - Follow each scholarship’s application instructions carefully.  

6. **Submit the university application**  
   - Most Australian universities use the *myUni* or *myUni* portal.  
   - Ensure you meet all deadlines and submit required documents.  

7. **Obtain a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)**  
   - Once accepted, the university will issue a CoE, which is needed for the student visa.  

8. **Apply for the student visa**  
   - Use the *ImmiAccount* portal to lodge the visa application.  
   - Include the CoE, proof of funds, OSHC, and any other required documents.  

9. **Plan for arrival**  
   - Arrange accommodation, transport, and health insurance.  
   - Register with the university’s international student office upon arrival.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Assuming all scholarships are open**  
   - Scholarship windows close at different times. Verify deadlines on the official scholarship page.  

2. **Submitting incomplete documents**  
   - Missing signatures, translations, or notarisation can delay or reject an application.  

3. **Ignoring visa conditions**  
   - Failing to meet health, financial, or English requirements can lead to visa refusal.  

4. **Overlooking support services**  
   - Many universities offer free orientation and support for refugees; not using these resources can make the transition harder.  

5. **Not planning for accommodation**  
   - Arranging housing before arrival can reduce stress and help you settle quickly.  

6. **Failing to keep records**  
   - Maintain copies of all applications, receipts, and correspondence for future reference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can refugees apply for a student visa in Australia?

Yes. Refugees holding a valid refugee or humanitarian visa can apply for a student visa (subclass 500) once they have an offer from an Australian education provider and meet the visa requirements.

### 2. Are there scholarships specifically for refugees?

Yes. The Australia Awards Scholarship and the Australian Government Research Training Program (AGRTP) are examples of scholarships that explicitly welcome refugees and humanitarian entrants. Many universities also offer scholarships that target refugees.

### 3. What English language test is required for most Australian universities?

Most universities accept IELTS (overall band 5.5 or higher), TOEFL iBT (overall score 61 or higher), or equivalent tests. Some may accept alternative evidence such as completion of an English language course.

### 4. Do I need to pay for health insurance separately?

Yes. Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory for all international students, including refugees, and must be purchased before or upon arrival in Australia.

### 5. How long does the student visa application process take?

Processing times vary. Generally, it can take several weeks to a few months. It is advisable to apply as early as possible after receiving your Confirmation of Enrolment.

### 6. Can I study part‑time while on a student visa?

Yes. Student visa holders can study part‑time (up to 40 hours per fortnight) during semester breaks and full‑time during semesters, subject to visa conditions.
