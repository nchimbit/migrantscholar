## Eligibility and Important Requirements

### 1. Citizenship and Nationality

Chevening requires applicants to be a **citizen of a Chevening‑eligible country**. The list of eligible countries is published on the Chevening website and is updated annually. Refugees who hold a passport from a country on that list are eligible.  

**Key points for refugees**

- If you are a refugee but still hold a passport from your country of origin, you can apply.  
- If you have lost your passport or are stateless, you must check whether Chevening accepts alternative travel documents (e.g., a refugee travel document). The official site will specify whether such documents are acceptable.  
- If you have acquired citizenship in another country, you may be eligible under that new nationality, provided it is on the Chevening list.

### 2. Academic Qualifications

- You must have a **good undergraduate degree** (or equivalent) that qualifies you for admission to a master’s programme in the UK.  
- The degree should be from a recognised institution and meet the academic standards of the UK university you intend to attend.  
- Chevening does not set a minimum GPA, but your academic record should demonstrate that you can succeed in a postgraduate environment.

### 3. Leadership Potential

Chevening looks for future leaders who will contribute to their home countries or the wider world. For refugees, this means:

- Demonstrating a track record of community engagement, advocacy, or other leadership activities.  
- Articulating a clear vision of how you will use your UK education to create positive change.  
- Providing evidence of past leadership roles (e.g., volunteer work, organising events, leading projects).

### 4. Return‑to‑Home Commitment

Chevening requires that recipients **return to their home country** (or the country where they hold citizenship) for at least two years after completing their studies. For refugees:

- If you are a refugee who has not yet returned to your country of origin, you must explain how you plan to meet this requirement.  
- If you intend to remain in the UK or another country after graduation, you must clarify your post‑study plans and how they align with Chevening’s expectations.

### 5. English Language Proficiency

- You must meet the English language requirements of the UK university you plan to attend.  
- Chevening itself does not impose a separate English test requirement, but you will need IELTS, TOEFL, or an equivalent score to gain admission.

### 6. Application Timing

- Chevening applications are typically open from **late September to early January** each year.  
- The exact dates vary; always check the official website for the current cycle’s deadlines.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

Chevening Scholarships are **fully funded** for the duration of the master’s programme. The funding package generally includes:

- **Tuition fee coverage** for the entire course.  
- **Monthly stipend** for living expenses (the amount is set by the UK government and may vary by region).  
- **Round‑trip airfare** to and from the UK.  
- **Travel allowance** for local travel within the UK.  
- **Health insurance** and other incidental costs.

Because the exact figures can change, consult the Chevening website for the most recent funding details. If you are a refugee, you may also be eligible for additional support from UK universities or charitable organisations that assist displaced students; check with the specific university’s international student office.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Identify Your Target Course and University

- Use the UK university course finder tools to locate master’s programmes that match your academic interests.  
- Verify that the university accepts Chevening applicants and that the course is eligible for the scholarship.

### 2. Confirm Chevening Eligibility

- Cross‑check your nationality against the Chevening list.  
- Verify that you possess a valid passport or travel document accepted by Chevening.  
- Ensure you meet the academic and leadership criteria.

### 3. Gather Academic Credentials

- Obtain official transcripts and degree certificates.  
- If your documents are not in English, arrange for certified translations.

### 4. Build a Strong Personal Statement

- Highlight your academic achievements, leadership experiences, and future goals.  
- Explain why the UK and the specific course are essential for your career path.  
- Emphasise how you will contribute to your community or country after graduation.

### 5. Secure References

- Request letters from professors, employers, or community leaders who can attest to your academic abilities and leadership potential.  
- Provide referees with a brief of what Chevening values so they can tailor their letters.

### 6. Prepare a Detailed CV

- Include academic history, work experience, volunteer activities, and any relevant skills.  
- Keep the CV concise (ideally one page) but comprehensive.

### 7. Arrange English Proficiency Proof

- Schedule and take an IELTS or TOEFL test if required by your chosen university.  
- Keep the official score report ready for submission.

### 8. Complete the Chevening Online Application

- Create an account on the Chevening portal.  
- Fill in personal details, academic background, and career aspirations.  
- Upload all required documents (passport copy, transcripts, CV, personal statement, references).  
- Pay attention to file size limits and acceptable formats.

### 9. Review and Submit

- Double‑check that all sections are complete and that documents are legible.  
- Submit before the deadline.  
- After submission, you will receive a confirmation email; keep it for your records.

### 10. Prepare for the Interview

- If shortlisted, you will be invited to an interview (often via video call).  
- Practice answering questions about your leadership, career plans, and why you chose the UK.  
- Be ready to discuss how you will meet the return‑to‑home requirement.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Applying with an ineligible nationality** – Double‑check the Chevening list before starting the application.  
2. **Submitting incomplete documents** – Missing transcripts or reference letters can lead to automatic rejection.  
3. **Weak personal statement** – Failing to connect your goals with Chevening’s mission reduces your chances.  
4. **Ignoring the return‑to‑home clause** – Not addressing how you will return after study can raise red flags.  
5. **Late submission** – Even a few hours past the deadline can disqualify you.  
6. **Not tailoring references** – Generic letters are less persuasive than those that speak to your leadership and impact.  
7. **Overlooking visa requirements** – Chevening covers tuition and living costs, but you still need a UK student visa.  
8. **Assuming funding covers everything** – Some incidental costs (e.g., travel to conferences) may not be included.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can refugees apply for Chevening Scholarships?

Yes, refugees who hold a passport from a Chevening‑eligible country and meet the other eligibility criteria can apply. If you only have a refugee travel document, check the official Chevening website to confirm whether it is accepted.

### 2. What documents do refugees need to prove their identity?

A valid passport is the primary document. If you do not have a passport, you may need to provide a refugee travel document or other government‑issued ID that is accepted by Chevening. Verify the acceptable documents on the official site.

### 3. Do I need a passport to apply?

Chevening requires a valid passport or equivalent travel document for the application. If you lack a passport, you must confirm that your alternative document is acceptable before proceeding.

### 4. What if I don’t have a passport or travel document?

If you are stateless or have lost your passport, you should contact the Chevening team or your local embassy for guidance. They can advise whether your situation is eligible and what alternative documents may be accepted.

### 5. Will Chevening cover my travel costs to the UK?

Chevening provides a round‑trip airfare allowance. The exact amount and conditions are listed on the official website. Make sure to read the travel policy carefully.

### 6. What are the visa requirements for Chevening recipients?

Chevening recipients must apply for a UK Tier 4 (Student) visa. The scholarship covers tuition and living costs, but you are responsible for the visa application fee and any related costs. Check the UK Home Office website for the latest visa rules.
