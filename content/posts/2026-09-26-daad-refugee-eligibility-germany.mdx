## Who This Guide Is For

This guide is written for refugees, asylum seekers, and international students who are currently residing in Germany and are interested in pursuing a Master’s or PhD program funded by the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD). It does **not** cover:

- Scholarships for non‑refugee international students who are studying in Germany on a different visa type.
- DAAD programmes that are specifically for German citizens or EU nationals.
- Detailed visa or immigration advice beyond what is required for scholarship eligibility.

If you are unsure whether you qualify as a refugee or asylum seeker, consult the German Federal Office for Migration and Refugees (BAMF) or a legal advisor.

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

### 1. General DAAD Eligibility

- **Nationality**: DAAD scholarships are open to students from all countries, including refugees and asylum seekers.
- **Residence Status**: Applicants must hold a valid residence permit in Germany that allows them to study. Refugees and asylum seekers typically have a “Residence Permit for Refugees” (Aufenthaltstitel für Flüchtlinge) or a temporary residence permit for asylum seekers (Aufenthaltstitel für Asylbewerber).
- **Academic Background**: A recognized secondary school diploma or an equivalent qualification that allows entry into a German university program. For PhD programmes, a Master’s degree or an equivalent is usually required.
- **Language Proficiency**: Depending on the programme, proof of German (e.g., TestDaF, DSH) or English (e.g., IELTS, TOEFL) proficiency may be required. Some DAAD scholarships are offered in English‑taught programmes.
- **Health Insurance**: Proof of valid health insurance coverage in Germany is mandatory for all scholarship applicants.

### 2. Refugee‑Specific Considerations

- **Proof of Refugee Status**: Applicants should be able to provide documentation confirming their refugee or asylum status, such as a residence permit, a refugee card, or an official letter from BAMF.
- **Special DAAD Initiatives**: Occasionally, DAAD runs special programmes aimed at refugees, such as the “DAAD Refugee Scholarship” or “DAAD Refugee Programme.” These programmes may have additional criteria, such as a demonstrated commitment to community service or a specific field of study related to humanitarian work. Check the official website for the most recent initiatives.

### 3. Application Timing

- **Application Periods**: DAAD scholarship application windows vary by programme. Some programmes accept applications year‑round, while others have fixed deadlines (e.g., January–March for fall intake). Always verify the exact dates on the DAAD website.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

DAAD scholarships for refugees typically cover:

- **Tuition Fees**: Full coverage of tuition (if the university charges tuition).
- **Living Allowance**: A monthly stipend to cover accommodation, food, transport, and study materials. The amount is usually around €850–€1,000 per month, but exact figures differ by programme.
- **Travel Allowance**: A one‑time travel grant for the return journey to the home country after graduation, if applicable.
- **Health Insurance**: Some programmes include a contribution to health insurance, but applicants usually need to maintain their own coverage.

Because each DAAD scholarship has its own funding structure, you should consult the specific programme page for precise details.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Identify the Right Programme

- Use the DAAD search tool to filter by “refugee” or “asylum seeker” in the eligibility section.
- Read the programme description carefully to confirm that it accepts refugees and to understand the specific field of study and language requirements.

### 2. Gather Academic Credentials

- Transcripts, diplomas, and certificates translated into German or English.
- Proof of prior academic achievements (e.g., a Master’s thesis abstract).

### 3. Prepare Language Proof

- Schedule and take the required language test (TestDaF, DSH, IELTS, TOEFL) well before the application deadline.

### 4. Secure Refugee Status Documentation

- Obtain a copy of your residence permit, refugee card, or BAMF letter confirming your status.

### 5. Draft a Strong Motivation Letter

- Highlight your academic goals, how the scholarship aligns with your refugee experience, and your future plans in Germany or your home country.
- Emphasize any community or humanitarian work you have undertaken.

### 6. Obtain Recommendation Letters

- Request letters from professors, employers, or community leaders who can attest to your academic potential and character.

### 7. Compile Health Insurance Proof

- Provide a copy of your health insurance card or a statement from your insurer confirming coverage.

### 8. Review the Application Checklist

- Double‑check that all required documents are included.
- Ensure that all documents are in the correct format (PDF, scanned copies, etc.).

### 9. Submit Early

- Even though deadlines vary, it is wise to submit your application at least 2–3 weeks before the stated deadline to allow for processing time.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Submitting Incomplete Applications**  
   Missing a single document can lead to automatic rejection. Use the programme’s checklist to verify completeness.

2. **Late Language Test Results**  
   Language test scores expire after a certain period (often 2 years). Ensure your scores are current at the time of application.

3. **Not Tailoring the Motivation Letter**  
   A generic letter may not convey how your refugee experience aligns with the scholarship’s objectives. Personalize each letter.

4. **Ignoring Visa and Health Insurance Requirements**  
   Even if you are a refugee, you must still comply with German health insurance regulations. Failing to provide proof can halt the application.

5. **Overlooking Programme‑Specific Criteria**  
   Some DAAD scholarships target specific fields (e.g., social sciences, engineering). Verify that your intended study area matches the programme.

6. **Assuming All Scholarships Cover Tuition**  
   While many DAAD scholarships cover tuition, some may only provide a stipend. Check the funding details for each programme.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. **Can a refugee apply for a DAAD scholarship if I am currently studying in Germany on a student visa?**  
Yes, refugees who hold a valid residence permit that allows them to study in Germany can apply. The key requirement is that the residence permit reflects your refugee or asylum status.

### 2. **Do I need to have a German university admission before applying for a DAAD scholarship?**  
Most DAAD scholarships require that you have a confirmed admission offer from a German university. Some programmes allow you to apply before admission, but you must secure admission before the scholarship period starts.

### 3. **What if I do not speak German?**  
Many DAAD scholarships are available for English‑taught programmes. If the scholarship requires German, you must provide proof of German proficiency (e.g., TestDaF, DSH). Check the specific language requirement for each programme.

### 4. **Will the DAAD scholarship cover my travel expenses to Germany?**  
Some DAAD programmes include a travel allowance for the return journey to your home country after graduation. However, most scholarships do not cover the initial travel to Germany. You may need to arrange this separately.

### 5. **Can I apply for multiple DAAD scholarships at the same time?**  
You can apply for more than one scholarship, but you must ensure that the deadlines do not conflict and that you meet the eligibility criteria for each. Be mindful of the time and effort required to prepare multiple applications.

### 6. **What happens if my refugee status changes during my studies?**  
If your status changes (e.g., from asylum seeker to recognized refugee), you should inform the DAAD and your university immediately. The scholarship terms may remain unchanged, but you must keep all documentation up to date.
