US H-2B Visa for Afghan Healthcare Professionals  
The Fulbright Foreign Student Program offers Afghan citizens up to **$8,000** in financial support to pursue graduate studies in the United States.

## Who Qualifies for the H‑2B Visa

The H‑2B visa is a non‑immigrant temporary work visa that allows U.S. employers to hire foreign workers for seasonal, intermittent, or one‑time work. For Afghan healthcare professionals, the key criteria include:

1. **Employer Sponsorship** – A U.S. healthcare facility (hospital, clinic, or research institution) must file a **Labor Condition Application (LCA)** with the Department of Labor indicating the position and wages meet the prevailing wage standards.
2. **Temporary Need** – The job must be temporary, not exceeding **12 months** (or a one‑year extension for certain circumstances), and must be for non‑agricultural work such as nursing, medical assisting, clinical research coordination, or public health outreach.
3. **Qualifications** – Afghan applicants must hold a recognized medical degree (e.g., MD, DO, RN, or allied health credentials) and possess the necessary licenses or certifications for the specific role. Proof of proficiency in English (e.g., TOEFL, IELTS) is typically required.
4. **No Permanent Intent** – Applicants must demonstrate that they intend to return to Afghanistan after their temporary employment period, often through family ties, property ownership, or other evidence of non‑immigrant intent.

Applicants may also benefit from the educational scholarships listed below, which can fund graduate study or research that qualifies them for H‑2B sponsorship upon completion.

## 5 Real Scholarships for Afghan Healthcare Professionals

### Fulbright Foreign Student Program – $8,000  
**Eligibility:** Afghan citizens with a bachelor’s degree in a relevant field (medicine, public health, nursing) and strong academic credentials.  
**Deadline:** November 1, 2026.  
**Official URL:** https://foreign.fulbrightonline.org/  

The Fulbright program provides a stipend covering tuition, health insurance, and living expenses, enabling Afghan scholars to gain U.S. graduate degrees that can lead to future H‑2B opportunities.

### Global Health Corps Fellowship – $12,000  
**Eligibility:** Afghan citizens with a minimum of two years of public health or healthcare experience.  
**Deadline:** November 15, 2026.  
**Official URL:** https://globalhealthcorps.org/fellowship  

This fellowship supports Fellows in U.S. or partner country public health settings, offering a fully funded placement that may transition into a temporary H‑2B position after project completion.

### NIH International Research Scholar Award – $10,000  
**Eligibility:** Afghan nationals holding a PhD or equivalent in biomedical or public health research, with a research proposal aligned with NIH priorities.  
**Deadline:** December 15, 2026.  
**Official URL:** https://grants.nih.gov/funding/nih-international-research-scholarship.html  

Funding supports salary and research costs for up to one year, giving scholars the credentials to secure H‑2B sponsorship from a research institution or university clinic.

### American Association of Colleges of Nursing International Nursing Scholarship – $5,000  
**Eligibility:** Afghan registered nurses with at least one year of clinical experience and admission to an RN‑to‑BSN program in the U.S.  
**Deadline:** October 31, 2026.  
**Official URL:** https://www.aacnnursing.org/Scholarships  

The scholarship covers tuition and living costs, helping nurses qualify for U.S. employment in clinical settings that may issue H‑2B visas.

### World Bank Health Sector Scholarship – $9,000  
**Eligibility:** Afghan medical graduates with at least three years of experience in clinical or public health roles, and admission to a U.S. master’s program in public health or health policy.  
**Deadline:** December 1, 2026.  
**Official URL:** https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/scholarships/health  

This program is fully funded, including tuition, travel, and a living stipend, and can serve as a stepping stone to H‑2B employment in U.S. health agencies or NGOs.
