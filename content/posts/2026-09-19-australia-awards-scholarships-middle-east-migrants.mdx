## Who This Guide Is For

This guide is written for migrants, refugees, asylum seekers, and international students from Middle Eastern countries who are interested in pursuing a Master’s degree in Australia through the Australia Awards Scholarship. It does **not** cover:

- Australian citizens or permanent residents applying for other Australian scholarships.  
- PhD‑level Australia Awards (unless explicitly stated on the official site).  
- Private or university‑specific scholarships that are not part of the Australia Awards Programme.

If you are unsure whether your country is a participating partner, check the official Australia Awards page or contact the nearest Australian High Commission.

---

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

### 1. Country Participation

Australia Awards Scholarships are offered to citizens of **participating countries**. The list of Middle Eastern partners changes over time. For the most accurate list, visit the official Australia Awards page.  

Typical participating countries include:

- Iraq  
- Jordan  
- Lebanon  
- Palestine  
- Syria (for certain programmes)  
- Yemen  

If your country is not listed, you are not eligible for the Australia Awards Programme.

### 2. Academic Criteria

- **Bachelor’s degree** (or equivalent) from a recognised institution.  
- Minimum academic performance (often a “good” or “very good” grade point average).  
- Subject relevance: the chosen Master’s course must align with the country’s development priorities (e.g., health, education, agriculture).  

### 3. Professional Experience

Some programmes require **at least two years of relevant work experience** after graduation. This is more common for programmes aimed at mid‑career professionals.

### 4. Language Proficiency

- **English language** proficiency is mandatory. Accepted tests include IELTS, TOEFL, or equivalent.  
- Minimum scores are usually set by the host university, not the Australia Awards itself.  

### 5. Commitment to Return

Applicants must demonstrate a **commitment to return** to their home country upon completion of the study. This is assessed through:

- A clear career plan that ties the Master’s degree to national development.  
- Evidence of ties to the community (e.g., family, employment prospects).  

### 6. Other General Requirements

- **Good health** and a clean criminal record.  
- **No prior Australia Awards** (unless you are applying for a different level).  

Because the exact wording and thresholds can vary, always read the country‑specific eligibility page on the Australia Awards website.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

The Australia Awards Scholarship is a **fully funded** award. The support typically includes:

- **Tuition fees** paid directly to the university.  
- **Living allowance** to cover accommodation, food, transport, and study materials.  
- **International travel** (economy class) to and from Australia.  
- **Health insurance** (Overseas Student Health Cover).  

The exact amounts are set by the Australian Government and can change. For the latest figures, consult the official Australia Awards page.

## How to Prepare an Application

Below are general steps that apply to all applicants, followed by a brief outline of the official application process.

### 1. Self‑Assessment

| Step | What to Do | Why |
|------|------------|-----|
| Identify your country | Check if your country is a partner | Eligibility hinges on this |
| Choose a university | Look for Australian universities offering your desired Master’s | Australia Awards partners with specific institutions |
| Verify course relevance | Ensure the course aligns with your country’s development priorities | Required by the scholarship |
| Check language requirements | Take an English test if you haven’t already | Needed for admission |

### 2. Academic and Professional Preparation

- **Maintain a strong GPA**; keep transcripts ready.  
- **Gain relevant work experience** if required.  
- **Collect recommendation letters** from academic supervisors or employers.  

### 3. Draft Your Personal Statement

- Outline your **career goals** and how the Master’s will help your country.  
- Highlight **community ties** and a clear plan to return.  
- Keep it concise (usually 500–800 words).  

### 4. Gather Supporting Documents

- Passport copy  
- Academic transcripts  
- Work experience certificates  
- English test scores  
- Recommendation letters  
- CV/Resume  

### 5. Register with the Australian High Commission

- Many countries require you to register with the local Australian High Commission or Embassy.  
- This step often involves a short interview or submission of a registration form.  

### 6. Apply to the University

- Submit your application to the chosen university’s admissions office.  
- Include the scholarship reference number if provided by the High Commission.  

### 7. Submit the Australia Awards Application

- Once the university accepts you, the High Commission will forward your details to the Australia Awards Secretariat.  
- The Secretariat will review your application against the scholarship criteria.  

### 8. Await Decision

- If successful, you will receive an official award letter.  
- Follow the instructions for visa application and pre‑departure briefing.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It’s Problematic | How to Fix It |
|---------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Applying to a non‑partner university** | The scholarship only funds partner institutions | Verify the university list on the Australia Awards site |
| **Submitting a weak personal statement** | The statement is a key assessment tool | Seek feedback from mentors or use university writing centres |
| **Missing the language requirement** | Universities will reject your application | Take the test early and aim for the required score |
| **Failing to show a return plan** | The scholarship emphasises national development | Include a clear, realistic career path |
| **Late submission of documents** | Deadlines are strict and non‑extendable | Use a checklist and submit well before the deadline |
| **Ignoring the High Commission registration** | Some countries require this step | Contact your local Australian Embassy for guidance |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. **Can refugees or asylum seekers from the Middle East apply for Australia Awards Scholarships?**  
The Australia Awards Programme is open to citizens of participating countries. Refugees or asylum seekers who are citizens of those countries may apply, but they must meet all standard eligibility criteria. If you are a refugee who has lost your citizenship status, you should consult the Australian High Commission for guidance.

### 2. **What if my country is not listed as a partner?**  
If your country is not on the current partner list, you are not eligible for the Australia Awards. However, you can still explore other Australian scholarships such as the Endeavour or Commonwealth Scholarships. Check the official Australia Awards page for updates.

### 3. **Do I need to apply through the Australian High Commission or directly to the university?**  
The process typically involves both. You first apply to the university; once accepted, the High Commission forwards your details to the Australia Awards Secretariat. Some countries may require an initial registration with the High Commission before university application.

### 4. **Is the scholarship renewable if I need a longer study period?**  
Australia Awards Scholarships are usually awarded for the duration of the Master’s program (typically 1–2 years). Extensions are rare and would require a separate application and justification. Check the official website for any specific renewal policies.

### 5. **What happens if I fail my English test after being accepted?**  
If you fail to meet the required English proficiency after acceptance, the scholarship may be withdrawn. Some universities offer English bridging courses, but the scholarship funds are generally tied to the main program. Contact the university’s admissions office for options.

### 6. **Can I study part‑time or take a gap year?**  
The scholarship is designed for full‑time study. Part‑time study or extended gaps are not typically supported. If you need to pause your studies, you must discuss this with the scholarship office and the university, but the scholarship may not cover the gap period.
