## Who This Guide Is For

- **Refugees, asylum seekers, and international students** who are currently residing in the UK and wish to pursue a master’s degree funded by the UK government.  
- **Citizens of a Chevening‑eligible country** who hold a valid UK visa or residence status (e.g., refugee status, asylum seeker, or other protected status).  
- **Applicants who are unsure** whether their current status allows them to apply; this guide clarifies the key points.

*Limitations:*  
The information below is based on the most recent publicly available guidelines from Chevening. Because eligibility rules can change, you should verify all details on the official Chevening website before starting your application.

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

### 1. Citizenship

- You must be a **citizen of a Chevening‑eligible country**. The list of eligible countries is published on the Chevening website and is updated annually.  
- Refugees who are citizens of a country on that list are eligible, regardless of their current residence status.

### 2. UK Visa or Residence Status

- Chevening requires applicants to have a **valid UK visa or residence status**. This includes:
  - Refugee status
  - Asylum seeker status
  - Other protected status (e.g., humanitarian protection)
- You must be able to provide evidence of this status when you apply.

### 3. Academic Qualifications

- You need a **first‑degree qualification** (or equivalent) that is recognized by the UK higher‑education system.  
- The degree should be relevant to the master’s programme you intend to pursue.

### 4. Professional Experience

- Chevening looks for **at least two years of relevant work experience**.  
- Experience can be in the public or private sector, or in a non‑profit setting.  
- For refugees, experience gained in the host country or through community work can be considered.

### 5. Leadership Potential

- Applicants must demonstrate **leadership qualities** and a clear plan for how they will use the scholarship to contribute to their home country or community after graduation.

### 6. English Language Proficiency

- You must meet the **English language requirements** for the chosen university.  
- Chevening itself does not set a separate English test score, but the university will.

### 7. Other General Criteria

- You must be **eligible to study in the UK** under the university’s admission rules.  
- You must be able to secure a place at a UK university that offers a Chevening‑eligible master’s programme.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

Chevening provides a **fully funded** scholarship package that typically includes:

- **Tuition fees** paid directly to the university.  
- **Monthly stipend** to cover living expenses.  
- **Round‑trip airfare** to and from the UK.  
- **Travel allowance** for local travel during the course of the study.  
- **Health insurance** coverage for the duration of the scholarship.

The exact figures for the stipend and travel allowance are set by the UK government and can vary each funding cycle. For the most accurate numbers, consult the Chevening website or contact the Chevening office in your country.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Identify a Chevening‑eligible Programme

- Browse the Chevening website’s list of partner universities and programmes.  
- Ensure the programme aligns with your career goals and the leadership impact you plan to make.

### 2. Confirm Your Eligibility

- Verify that your country is on the Chevening‑eligible list.  
- Check that your UK visa or residence status is valid and can be documented.

### 3. Gather Academic and Professional Evidence

- Obtain official transcripts and degree certificates.  
- Prepare a detailed CV that highlights relevant work experience and leadership roles.

### 4. Draft Your Personal Statement

- The personal statement is a critical component.  
- Focus on:
  - Your motivation for the chosen field.  
  - How the scholarship will help you achieve your goals.  
  - The impact you intend to create in your community or country.

### 5. Secure References

- Request letters of recommendation from supervisors, professors, or community leaders who can attest to your leadership and academic potential.

### 6. Prepare Supporting Documents

- Proof of UK visa or residence status.  
- Passport copy.  
- Any additional documents required by the university (e.g., portfolio, research proposal).

### 7. Submit the Application

- Complete the online application form on the Chevening portal.  
- Upload all required documents.  
- Pay attention to the application deadline and submit well before it closes.

### 8. Follow Up

- After submission, monitor your email for any requests for additional information.  
- If shortlisted, you may be invited for an interview.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Submitting an incomplete application** – Double‑check that every field is filled and every required document is uploaded.  
2. **Ignoring the personal statement guidelines** – The statement must be tailored to Chevening’s criteria; generic statements are unlikely to succeed.  
3. **Overlooking the visa status requirement** – Even if you are a refugee, you must have a valid UK visa or residence status that can be documented.  
4. **Failing to demonstrate leadership** – Provide concrete examples of leadership roles or initiatives you have led.  
5. **Missing the application deadline** – Chevening deadlines are strict; late applications are usually not considered.  
6. **Not verifying eligibility for the specific programme** – Some universities may have additional requirements that could disqualify you if not met.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can a refugee who is not a citizen of a Chevening‑eligible country apply?

No. Chevening requires applicants to be citizens of a country on the official Chevening‑eligible list. Refugees who are citizens of a non‑eligible country cannot apply, even if they hold refugee status in the UK.

### 2. Does having refugee status automatically grant me a Chevening scholarship?

Refugee status alone does not guarantee eligibility. You must also meet all other Chevening criteria, including citizenship, academic qualifications, work experience, and leadership potential.

### 3. What if my UK visa is about to expire during the scholarship period?

You will need to ensure your visa or residence status remains valid for the entire duration of the scholarship. Chevening does not cover visa renewal costs, so plan accordingly and consult the UK Home Office for guidance.

### 4. Are there any additional costs I should anticipate?

While Chevening covers tuition, living allowance, and travel, you may still need to pay for:
- Health insurance (if not covered by Chevening).  
- Optional travel or accommodation outside the scholarship period.  
- Any university fees not included in the scholarship (e.g., course materials).

### 5. How long does the application process take?

The process can take several months from the initial application to the final award decision. It is advisable to start early, gather documents, and submit well before the deadline.

### 6. Can I apply for Chevening if I am currently studying in the UK on a different visa?

Yes, as long as you hold a valid UK visa or residence status that meets Chevening’s requirements and you satisfy all other eligibility criteria. However, you must still apply through the Chevening portal and follow the standard application procedure.
