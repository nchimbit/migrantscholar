## Who This Guide Is For

This guide is written for refugees, asylum seekers, and international students who are considering a Master’s degree in Germany and are interested in a DAAD scholarship that may cover tuition, living expenses, and travel costs. It is **not** a legal or immigration advice resource. If you have questions about visa status, residency permits, or legal rights, consult a qualified immigration lawyer or the German embassy/consulate in your country.

---

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

DAAD (the German Academic Exchange Service) offers several scholarship programmes that are open to refugees and asylum seekers. While the overarching goal is to support international students, each scholarship has its own set of criteria. In general, you should verify the following:

1. **Refugee status** – You must hold a valid refugee status, asylum seeker status, or an equivalent protection status recognized by the German authorities.  
2. **Academic background** – A completed bachelor’s degree (or equivalent) in a relevant field, with a minimum GPA or grade point average that meets the programme’s standard.  
3. **Language proficiency** – Depending on the programme, you may need to demonstrate proficiency in German (e.g., TestDaF, DSH) or English (e.g., IELTS, TOEFL).  
4. **Motivation and research fit** – A clear statement of purpose that aligns with the chosen university’s research focus.  
5. **Health insurance** – Proof of health insurance coverage that meets German requirements.  

Because each scholarship has its own nuances, always read the specific programme description on the DAAD website. If any requirement is unclear, contact the DAAD scholarship office or the university’s international office for clarification.

---

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

The DAAD offers full funding for many Master’s programmes, which typically includes:

- **Tuition fee waiver** – Most public German universities do not charge tuition for Master’s students, but some private institutions do. The scholarship usually covers any tuition that may be required.  
- **Monthly stipend** – A living allowance that varies by city and programme, intended to cover accommodation, food, transport, and study materials.  
- **Health insurance** – Coverage for the duration of the scholarship period.  
- **Travel allowance** – A one‑time or periodic reimbursement for travel to and from Germany.  

The exact amounts and the duration of the stipend depend on the specific scholarship and the university. Check the DAAD website for the most recent figures.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Identify the Right Scholarship

- Visit the DAAD scholarship portal and filter by “Refugees” or “Asylum seekers.”  
- Read the programme descriptions carefully, noting the application window, required documents, and selection criteria.  

### 2. Contact the University

- Reach out to the international office of the university you wish to attend.  
- Ask about the Master’s programme’s admission requirements, language prerequisites, and whether the university participates in the DAAD refugee scholarship.  

### 3. Gather Academic Credentials

- Obtain official transcripts and degree certificates.  
- If your documents are not in German or English, arrange for certified translations.  

### 4. Prepare Language Proof

- Schedule and take the required language test (German or English).  
- Keep the official score reports ready for submission.  

### 5. Draft a Strong Motivation Letter

- Explain why you want to study in Germany, how the programme aligns with your career goals, and how your background as a refugee adds a unique perspective.  
- Highlight any research experience or projects relevant to the field.  

### 6. Secure Recommendation Letters

- Request letters from professors, employers, or community leaders who can attest to your academic abilities and personal resilience.  
- Provide them with a brief on the scholarship’s focus so they can tailor their letters.  

### 7. Compile the Application Package

- Follow the DAAD application checklist: personal data, academic records, language certificates, motivation letter, CV, recommendation letters, proof of refugee status, health insurance, and any additional documents requested by the university.  

### 8. Submit Early

- Even though you cannot guarantee a deadline, aim to submit at least 4–6 weeks before the stated application window closes.  
- Keep copies of all submitted documents and confirmation receipts.  

### 9. Prepare for Interviews (if required)

- Some scholarships may require an interview with the DAAD selection committee or the university.  
- Practice discussing your research interests, career plans, and how you will contribute to the academic community.  

### 10. Follow Up

- After submission, monitor your email for any requests for additional information.  
- If you do not receive a response within the expected timeframe, politely inquire about the status of your application.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Submitting incomplete applications** – Missing a single document can lead to automatic rejection.  
2. **Late submissions** – Even if the scholarship is still open, late applications are often not considered.  
3. **Using informal language** – A professional tone in your motivation letter and CV is essential.  
4. **Failing to tailor the application** – Generic statements do not resonate with selection committees.  
5. **Ignoring language requirements** – Some programmes require a minimum score; do not assume a lower score will suffice.  
6. **Not verifying visa eligibility** – Even with a scholarship, you must still meet German visa requirements.  
7. **Overlooking health insurance** – Proof of coverage is mandatory; otherwise, your application may be incomplete.  
8. **Not following the DAAD application format** – Use the templates and guidelines provided on the DAAD website.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. **Can I apply for a DAAD scholarship if I am still in the asylum application process?**  
Yes, many DAAD programmes accept applicants who are currently seeking asylum or have a pending application. However, you must provide proof of your status or the status of your application. Check the specific scholarship’s eligibility section for details.

### 2. **Do I need to have a German language certificate to apply for a DAAD Master’s scholarship?**  
It depends on the programme. Some Master’s courses are taught entirely in English, while others require German proficiency. Review the language requirements listed for each scholarship and the university’s programme page.

### 3. **What happens if I am accepted but cannot secure a visa in time?**  
The scholarship covers tuition and living expenses, but you must still obtain a German student visa. If you cannot secure the visa before the start of the semester, the scholarship may be suspended or cancelled. Contact the DAAD scholarship office for guidance on visa timelines.

### 4. **Is the DAAD scholarship renewable if I need to extend my studies?**  
Renewal depends on the specific scholarship and the university’s policies. Some scholarships cover the full duration of a Master’s programme (typically 1–2 years), while others may allow extensions under certain conditions. Verify renewal options in the scholarship description.

### 5. **Can I use the DAAD scholarship to study at a private university?**  
Yes, DAAD offers scholarships for both public and private institutions. However, private universities may have higher tuition fees, which the scholarship may or may not cover. Confirm the tuition coverage details in the scholarship information.

### 6. **What support does DAAD provide after I arrive in Germany?**  
DAAD offers orientation events, networking opportunities, and sometimes mentorship programmes. Additionally, the university’s international office can help with accommodation, registration, and integration into campus life. Check the DAAD website for post‑arrival support services.
