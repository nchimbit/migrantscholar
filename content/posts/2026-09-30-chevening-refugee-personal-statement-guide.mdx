## Who This Guide Is For

This guide is written for refugees, asylum seekers, and displaced persons who are considering applying for a Chevening Scholarship to study a Master’s degree in the United Kingdom. It assumes you have a clear idea of the subject you wish to study and the UK university you are targeting. The guide focuses on the personal statement component of the application, which is a critical part of the selection process. It does **not** replace the official application instructions on the Chevening website, nor does it provide legal or immigration advice.

---

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

Chevening Scholarships are awarded to individuals who meet the following general criteria:

1. **Nationality** – You must be a national of a Chevening-eligible country. Refugees and asylum seekers are eligible if they hold a passport from a Chevening-eligible country or have been granted refugee status in a Chevening-eligible country.
2. **Academic Merit** – You should have a good academic record and a strong application for a UK Master’s programme.
3. **Leadership Potential** – Chevening seeks candidates who demonstrate leadership and a commitment to making a positive impact in their home country or community.
4. **Return Commitment** – You must intend to return to your home country (or the country where you are registered as a refugee) after completing your studies.

Because refugee status can affect how you meet these criteria, it is essential to:

- Verify that your country of origin or current residence is on the Chevening list of eligible countries.
- Confirm that your refugee or asylum status is recognised by the UK Home Office and that you can obtain a Tier 4 (General) student visa.
- Check that you meet the academic and language requirements for the specific Master’s programme you intend to pursue.

**Important:** The above points are general guidelines. For the most accurate and up‑to‑date information, always consult the official Chevening website and, if necessary, seek advice from the UK embassy or a qualified immigration adviser.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

Chevening Scholarships cover:

- Full tuition fees for the chosen Master’s programme
- Living allowance (monthly stipend)
- One return airfare to the UK and one return airfare to your home country
- Travel costs to and from the UK for the first and last weeks of the programme
- Health insurance and other incidental expenses

The exact amounts are set by the UK government and can change. Refugees and asylum seekers receive the same level of support as other Chevening scholars. For precise figures, refer to the official Chevening website.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Understand Chevening’s Core Themes

Chevening looks for candidates who can demonstrate:

- **Leadership** – Past or potential leadership roles, initiatives, or projects.
- **Networking** – Ability to build and maintain professional relationships.
- **Academic Excellence** – Strong academic record and clear research or study objectives.
- **Impact** – A concrete plan for how you will use your UK education to benefit your home country or community.

### 2. Map Your Refugee Experience to These Themes

Your personal journey can be a powerful illustration of resilience, adaptability, and commitment. Think of specific moments where you:

- Took initiative in a community setting
- Overcame obstacles that required creative problem‑solving
- Demonstrated empathy and cultural sensitivity

### 3. Draft a Structured Outline

| Section | What to Include |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Opening Hook** | A brief, vivid anecdote or statement that captures your motivation. |
| **Academic Background** | Key achievements, relevant coursework, and why you chose your field. |
| **Leadership & Impact** | Concrete examples of leadership, community work, or projects. |
| **Why the UK & Chevening** | Explain why a UK Master’s is essential for your goals and how Chevening aligns with your aspirations. |
| **Future Plan** | A clear, realistic plan for applying your knowledge after graduation. |
| **Closing** | Reiterate your commitment and gratitude. |

### 4. Write with Authenticity

- Use first‑person narrative; Chevening values genuine voices.
- Avoid clichés such as “I am a hard worker” without evidence.
- Show, don’t tell: illustrate your points with specific stories.

### 5. Seek Feedback

- Ask mentors, professors, or peers who understand the Chevening process to review your draft.
- If possible, find a refugee support organisation that offers writing workshops.

### 6. Polish and Proofread

- Check for clarity, flow, and conciseness.
- Ensure you stay within the word limit (usually 500–600 words for the personal statement).
- Eliminate grammatical errors and typos.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Weighs Against You | How to Fix It |
|---------|---------------------------|---------------|
| **Over‑emphasising hardship without showing growth** | Chevening wants to see resilience, not just victimhood. | Highlight how challenges led to specific skills or insights. |
| **Generic statements about “leadership”** | Vague claims lack impact. | Provide concrete examples with measurable outcomes. |
| **Ignoring the UK context** | Chevening seeks scholars who understand UK higher education. | Mention specific UK universities, faculty, or resources that align with your goals. |
| **Failing to link your future plan to Chevening’s objectives** | The scholarship is about impact. | Clearly state how your post‑study work will benefit your community or country. |
| **Wordiness and repetition** | Reduces readability and may exceed limits. | Edit ruthlessly; keep sentences concise. |
| **Submitting a statement that is too short** | May appear under‑prepared. | Aim for the upper end of the word limit, ensuring you cover all key points. |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can I apply for a Chevening Scholarship if I am currently a refugee in the UK?

Yes, refugees who meet Chevening’s eligibility criteria—including nationality, academic merit, and leadership potential—can apply. However, you must confirm that your refugee status is recognised by the UK Home Office and that you can obtain a Tier 4 (General) student visa. Check the official Chevening website for the latest eligibility details.

### 2. How should I address my refugee status in the personal statement?

Mention your status briefly and focus on how it has shaped your aspirations and resilience. Use it as a backdrop to illustrate your motivation, rather than as the sole focus of your narrative.

### 3. Are there any additional documents required for refugees?

In addition to the standard Chevening documents, you may need to provide a letter confirming your refugee or asylum status, issued by the relevant authority in your country of residence. Verify the exact documents with the Chevening application portal.

### 4. What if I have limited English proficiency?

Chevening requires a minimum English language score (e.g., IELTS 7.0 overall). If you are below the threshold, you can still apply by taking the required exam before the application deadline. Some universities also offer preparatory courses; check with your chosen institution.

### 5. How can I demonstrate leadership if I have limited formal experience?

Leadership can be shown through informal roles—organising community events, leading volunteer projects, or mentoring peers. Provide specific examples that illustrate initiative, responsibility, and impact.

### 6. Is there a deadline for the Chevening application cycle I should be aware of?

Chevening application deadlines vary each year and by country. Because the dates can change, always consult the official Chevening website for the most current information.
