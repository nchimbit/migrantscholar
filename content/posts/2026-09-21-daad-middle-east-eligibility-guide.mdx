## Who This Guide Is For

This guide is written for international students, refugees, and asylum seekers from the Middle East who are considering studying in Germany at the master’s or doctoral level. It focuses on the DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service) scholarships that are most commonly available to students in this region.  

**Limitations**  
- The information below is based on the official DAAD website and general scholarship guidelines.  
- Specific programmes may have additional or slightly different requirements.  
- Application periods, funding amounts, and eligibility criteria can change. Always verify the latest details on the DAAD website before you apply.

---

## Eligibility and Important Requirements

DAAD offers a range of scholarships, but most share a core set of eligibility criteria:

| Criterion | What it means for Middle Eastern applicants |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Academic record** | A strong university performance (usually a minimum GPA or class rank). For master’s programmes, a bachelor’s degree that is recognised in Germany. For PhD programmes, a master’s degree or equivalent. |
| **Language proficiency** | • German‑speaking programmes: a recognised German language certificate (e.g., TestDaF, DSH, Goethe‑Zertifikat). <br>• English‑speaking programmes: an English language certificate (e.g., IELTS, TOEFL, Cambridge). |
| **Research proposal** | For PhD and some master’s programmes, a clear research idea that aligns with the interests of a German university or research institute. |
| **Motivation and fit** | A statement of purpose that explains why you want to study in Germany and how the scholarship will help you achieve your goals. |
| **Legal status** | You must be able to obtain a German student visa. DAAD does not replace immigration procedures. |
| **Nationality** | DAAD scholarships are open to students from all countries, including those in the Middle East. Some programmes may have quotas or special provisions for certain regions. |

**Important**: Each scholarship may add its own specific requirements (e.g., a minimum number of years of study, a particular field of study, or a partnership with a German university). Check the programme page on the DAAD website for the exact criteria.

## What Funding or Support May Be Available

DAAD scholarships generally cover:

- **Tuition fee exemption** – Many German universities do not charge tuition for international students, but some programmes still require a small fee that the scholarship may cover.  
- **Monthly stipend** – A living allowance that varies by city and programme.  
- **Travel allowance** – A one‑time payment to cover travel to and from Germany.  
- **Health insurance** – Some scholarships include a contribution to health insurance.  

The exact amounts are programme‑specific and can change annually. For the most accurate figures, consult the scholarship description on the DAAD website.

## How to Prepare an Application

### 1. Identify the right scholarship

- Visit the DAAD website and use the “Scholarships” search tool.  
- Filter by “Country of origin” (Middle East) and “Study level” (Master’s or PhD).  
- Read the brief description of each scholarship to see if it matches your field of study and language preference.

### 2. Understand the timeline

- Each scholarship has its own application window.  
- Mark the dates on your calendar, but remember that the official deadlines are listed on the DAAD site.  
- Start early: language tests, recommendation letters, and research proposals often take weeks to prepare.

### 3. Gather academic credentials

- Official transcripts and degree certificates.  
- If your documents are not in German or English, arrange for certified translations.  
- Some programmes require a proof of recognition of your degree in Germany (e.g., via the “Anabin” database).

### 4. Prepare language certificates

- Book your language test well in advance.  
- Keep the official test result letter or a scanned copy ready for upload.

### 5. Draft a research proposal (if required)

- Outline your research question, methodology, and expected contribution.  
- Align it with the interests of a potential supervisor in Germany.  
- Keep the proposal concise (usually 1–2 pages for master’s, 3–5 pages for PhD).

### 6. Secure recommendation letters

- Ask professors or employers who know you well.  
- Provide them with a brief on the scholarship and the deadline.  
- Some programmes allow electronic submission; others require hard copies.

### 7. Write a motivation letter

- Explain why you chose Germany, the specific university, and how the scholarship will help you.  
- Highlight any previous international experience or projects that demonstrate your readiness.

### 8. Complete the online application

- Create a DAAD account if you don’t already have one.  
- Fill in personal details, upload documents, and pay any application fee (if required).  
- Double‑check that all files are in the correct format and size.

### 9. Submit before the deadline

- Send the application at least a few days before the official cut‑off to avoid technical issues.  
- Keep a confirmation email or screenshot as proof of submission.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Submitting incomplete applications** – Missing a single PDF can lead to automatic rejection.  
2. **Late language test results** – If your test expires before the application deadline, you’ll need to retake it.  
3. **Using informal language in motivation letters** – Keep the tone professional and concise.  
4. **Ignoring the scholarship’s specific focus** – Some programmes target certain disciplines or research areas; applying outside those areas reduces your chances.  
5. **Overlooking the visa process** – Even with a scholarship, you must still secure a student visa.  
6. **Not confirming the application receipt** – Save the confirmation email or screenshot; it’s proof that you applied on time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Can refugees from the Middle East apply for DAAD scholarships?

Yes, refugees and asylum seekers are eligible to apply for many DAAD scholarships, provided they meet the academic and language requirements. Some programmes may have additional provisions for refugees, but you should check the specific scholarship page for details.

### 2. Do I need to speak German to apply for a master’s programme?

It depends on the programme’s language of instruction. If the master’s is taught in German, you’ll need a recognised German language certificate. If it’s taught in English, an English language certificate suffices. Always verify the language requirement on the university’s website.

### 3. How long does the scholarship application process take?

The application window varies by scholarship, but the entire process—from gathering documents to submitting the application—can take several weeks. After submission, the review period may last from a few weeks to a few months. Check the scholarship’s timeline for specific dates.

### 4. Will the scholarship cover my travel expenses to Germany?

Many DAAD scholarships include a travel allowance, but the amount and whether it covers return travel can differ. Look for the “Travel allowance” section in the scholarship description.

### 5. If I’m accepted, do I still need to apply for a German student visa?

Yes. The scholarship does not replace the visa application. Once you receive the acceptance letter, you’ll need to apply for a student visa at the German embassy or consulate in your country.

### 6. Can I apply for multiple DAAD scholarships at once?

You can apply to more than one scholarship, but each application must be submitted separately and meet its own criteria. Be mindful of overlapping deadlines and the time required to prepare each application.
